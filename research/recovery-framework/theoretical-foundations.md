## Theoretical Foundations

The MCCR Recovery Framework draws on established research in psychology, neuroscience, behavioral medicine, health services research, systems science, and human-computer interaction. It synthesizes existing theory and adds several new constructs, offered as hypotheses for future testing rather than settled findings.

| Concept | Status | Grounded in | Key sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Patient Agency** | Established | Self-efficacy, patient activation, shared decision making, self-determination theory | Bandura (1977); Hibbard et al. (2004); Ryan & Deci (2000); Elwyn et al. (2012) |
| **Recovery Capacity** | Proposed synthesis | Resilience, allostatic load, chronic illness self-management | McEwen (1998); Bonanno (2004); Barlow et al. (2002) |
| **Co-regulation Networks** | Proposed synthesis | Attachment theory, social support, social network science | Bowlby (1969); Berkman & Glass (2000); Coan & Sbarra (2015) |
| **Institutional Friction** | Proposed construct | Administrative burden, treatment burden, structural competency | Herd & Moynihan (2018); May et al. (2009); Metzl & Hansen (2014) |
| **Source Displacement** | Proposed construct | Predictive processing, interoception, stress appraisal | Craig (2002); Friston (2010); Lazarus & Folkman (1984); Barrett (2017) |
| **Recovery as Distributed Process** | Conceptual synthesis | Ecological systems theory, distributed cognition, biopsychosocial medicine | Bronfenbrenner (1979); Hutchins (1995); Engel (1977) |
| **Explainable Recovery Insights** | Technological application | Explainable AI, human-centered AI design | Doshi-Velez & Kim (2017); Amershi et al. (2019) |

**How to read this table:** "Established" concepts rest directly on decades of prior research. Everything else is MCCR's own theoretical contribution, built by combining established ideas in a new way, and needs empirical validation before it can be treated as fact.
